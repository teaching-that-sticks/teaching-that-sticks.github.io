## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 90 minutes. Session 3 of 3, a week after Session 2 |
| **Group** | About 8–12 (works 6–20) |
| **Canvas boxes** | Outcomes → Evidence → Follow-through, then the whole canvas |
| **Running artefact** | The same session, now with outcomes, evidence and a follow-through plan |
| **Shape** | Try what you made, sort the evidence, rewrite the outcomes, check alignment, plan follow-through, then the reveal |
| **The commitment** | No card. The completed canvas, plus **one named person who will ask them about it at 30 days** |

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, participants will have:

1. **Sorted** evidence from a teaching programme by what it actually shows, from *delivered* to *impact*. *(Card sort)*
2. **Rewritten** one to three outcomes for their own session using verbs someone could observe. *(Verb wall · Rewrite)*
3. **Matched** each outcome to evidence at the right level of Miller's pyramid, and checked their slides and activity against it. *(Rewrite · Does it line up?)*
4. **Completed** a design canvas for their session, including a 30/60/90-day follow-through plan and a named person who will ask them about it. *(Follow-through · The reveal)*

## Before the session

### Who goes first

Ask 2–3 people during the week whether they're happy to run their Menti or Kahoot on the group. Check their links work.

### The card sort

One set per table (3–4 people). The case is a made-up composite: *"a four-day programme with photos, packs and eight commitments, and nobody knows what changed."* Never use a real programme.

| Card | Ladder | Kirkpatrick |
|---|---|---|
| Attendance list: 48 staff | Delivered | None |
| Photos from all four days | Delivered | None |
| Delegate packs handed out | Delivered | None |
| Feedback forms: average 4.8/5 | Delivered | L1 |
| "The room was freezing and I couldn't find the venue" | Delivered | L1 (its real use: fix the logistics) |
| Flipchart photo of eight commitments | Produced | None yet: an intention, not a change |
| End-of-day quiz scores | Produced | L2 |
| A handover checklist redesigned on day 3 | Produced | L2 |
| "Observed on the ward at 30 days" | Implemented | L3 |
| "Learners now do X" (manager, at 90 days) | Implemented | L3 |
| Fewer incidents in the area six months later | Impact | L4 (hard to attribute) |

Print the sort board: four rungs (*Delivered → Produced → Implemented → Impact*) across, Kirkpatrick L1–L4 down.

### Room and kit

- [ ] Blorps retest slips (and last week's tally)
- [ ] Card-sort sets and boards, one per table
- [ ] Verb wall: a sheet headed "Can you see it?" and sticky notes
- [ ] Miller's pyramid, drawn or on one slide
- [ ] Blank canvases (A3), one each, plus your own filled-in canvas for this programme, covered until the reveal
- [ ] Participants' Session 1 slides and Session 2 designs (they bring them; spares of the bad-slide pack)
- [ ] Session 3 feedback form, paper or Microsoft Forms ([Evaluation Toolkit](/facilitator/evaluation-toolkit))
- [ ] Visible timer; venue wi-fi tested, phone hotspot as back-up

## Session flow

### 0–10 · Blorps retest → try what you made

- **First, 2 minutes, unannounced:** hand out the blorps retest slips again. Score and tally Group A (re-read) vs Group B (self-test) next to last week's numbers. Expected: the self-testers hold up better over a week (Roediger & Karpicke 2006). Whatever the result, SAY: that was spaced retrieval, and how would you *know* something stuck? That's today.
- SAY: last week you planned to make something. Let's use it. 2–3 people run their Menti or Kahoot on the group, 2–3 minutes each.
- Not done? ASK: what got in the way? Bring the idea; we'll build on it.
- WHY: closes the Session 2 loop, puts the thing to real use, and retrieves Sessions 1–2 through their own questions. → [retrieval practice](/theories/retrieval-practice)
- FALLBACK: tech fails → the maker reads their questions aloud; room answers on fingers or paper.

### 10–22 · Delivered ≠ changed

- SAY: here's a made-up case: a four-day programme with photos, packs and eight commitments, and nobody knows what changed. Sort its evidence.
- Tables place each card on the ladder and against Kirkpatrick L1–L4. 6 minutes, then compare.
- SAY: counts show it *happened*, not that anything changed. L1 has a narrow, real use: the cold room, the venue nobody could find.
- REVEAL: what you did in the opener (using what you made) was *use*: Level 3-type evidence, inside the room.
- WHY: [Kirkpatrick](/theories/kirkpatrick-model), including the New World version's point that L3 depends on what happens after training.

### 22–35 · What is "it"? The verb wall

- ASK: what verbs do you see in learning outcomes? Collect them: know, understand, be aware of, appreciate.
- ASK: could you *see* someone "be aware of" something? Let the laughter happen.
- Swap each for a verb you could observe: explain, list, demonstrate, choose, escalate, redesign, justify.
- WHY: [constructive alignment](/theories/constructive-alignment) needs outcomes you can check. Verb lists come from revised Bloom (Anderson & Krathwohl 2001); Biggs & Tang use them for alignment.

### 35–40 · Verbs decide your evidence

- Show Miller's pyramid: knows → knows how → shows how → does.
- SAY: the verb picks the level. "Be aware of" can only be evidenced at the bottom. "Demonstrates" needs someone to watch. "Does" means in practice.
- WHY: links outcomes straight to evaluation. → [Miller's pyramid](/theories/millers-pyramid)

### 40–58 · Rewrite your session's outcomes

- SAY: your session. Write 1–3 outcomes, each with its evidence: what would you see, and when? Fill in the Outcomes and Evidence boxes. Pairs, then 2–3 share.
- ASK the pairs: can you see it? Does the evidence sit at the same level as the verb?
- WHY: constructive alignment in practice. Peer rewrites are the worked examples.
- FALLBACK: no session yet? Use "a session you'd like to teach".

### 58–65 · Does it line up?

- SAY: put last week's activity and your redesigned slides next to the new outcomes. Does each one help learners do the verb? Change what doesn't.
- WHY: Biggs again, now at session scale. Session 1 was slide scale.
- IF SHORT: halve this, never drop it.

### 65–75 · Follow-through loop

- SAY: forgetting is the default. Plan what *your learners* will revisit at 30, 60 and 90 days, and how (a Kahoot, an email, a team message).
- ASK: who will ask *you* about this at 30 days? Write their name in the Follow-through box.
- WHY: transfer depends on the work environment as much as the training (Baldwin & Ford 1988). The programme can't hold this loop after it ends, so it sits with the participant and someone in their world. → [spaced practice](/theories/spaced-practice)

### 75–87 · The reveal

**Protected. Never cut.**

- SAY: you've been on a canvas for three weeks. Here's ours. Uncover this programme's own canvas:

| Box | Teaching That Sticks |
|---|---|
| **Learners & context** | Anyone in health and care who teaches, or would like to. Busy, rota'd, mixed experience. 3 × 90 minutes, a week apart. |
| **Need** | Teaching planned as events: dense slides, "be aware of" outcomes, commitments nobody follows up, evaluation designed afterwards. Nobody knows what changed. |
| **Outcomes** | Redesign a slide to cut the noise. Make ten minutes of a session active with a named strategy. Write observable outcomes, each with matching evidence. Plan follow-through with a named person. |
| **Evidence** | Redesigned slides shown in Session 2. The Session 2 Kahoot. The Menti or Kahoot you made, run on this group. Your completed canvas. The 30- and 90-day check. |
| **Activities** | Experience → name it → use it: the bad slide, the adverts, two redesign rounds, Kahoot then retrieval, the Menti tour, your active ten minutes, the card sort, the verb wall, your rewrite. |
| **Follow-through** | An ask on a card each week, read back the next week. A week's spacing. Your named person at 30 days. The 30- and 90-day check. |

- SAY: the spine question, answered: what do we need people to do differently, and how will our teaching make that happen?
- Hand out a blank canvas. Participants copy their boxes onto one clean page and photograph it. That's the commitment.
- WHY: making the hidden design visible. The programme is the final worked example.
- Share [Before You Plan a Teaching Day](/facilitator/before-you-plan): the canvas as six questions, for managers and organisers.

### 87–90 · Close

- ASK: one sentence each, aloud: "The one thing I'll do differently is…"
- SAY: thank you. Your named person will ask; so will we, at 30 and 90 days.
- Hand out or link the Session 3 feedback form as people leave.
- IF SHORT: keep the sentences; send the form link afterwards.

## After the session

- **Nothing to read back next week.** The read-back is now each participant's named person at 30 days.
- **Level 2:** check completed canvases against the short rubric. Are outcomes observable verbs? Does the evidence match the verb?
- **Level 3:** at 30 and 90 days, send the three questions: *What did you change? What happened? What got in the way?*
- All forms, the rubric, timings and governance: [Evaluation Toolkit](/facilitator/evaluation-toolkit). Don't restate them here.
- Psychological safety, debriefing and feedback are parked for a separate simulation-based session.
